*Built with Sarah Rodrigues, Maddie, Helen, and Najja for Intro to Mechanical
Engineering (Ms. Selan) — the term's second project: a **life-sized,
human-powered wooden vehicle**, modeled in Fusion 360 and built from plywood and
2×4s under tight material limits.*

## From sketches to Fusion

Like the bridge, we began with sketches and then built the car in **Fusion
360**. Because we were working on the Fusion file on my computer, I ended up
**primarily responsible** for the model — most of the edits and iterations ran
through me, which I'm proud of.

## Steering: rope *and* foot

The one thing our group debated was how to steer. We weighed **Ackermann**,
**rope**, and **foot** steering; Maddie, Sarah, and I leaned toward foot
steering, while Helen and Najja liked rope. Instead of picking one, we **merged
them** — and it turned out to be the right call. The foot steering would have
worked on its own, but the ropes gave us far more nuanced control and made the
car genuinely easy to turn.

## Problem-solving under constraints

- **Working across two class sections** was harder than expected — we started a
  group chat and sent an end-of-class summary each day so everyone stayed on the
  same page.
- **Sharing materials:** when other groups ran short on bolts, I came in after
  class to add extra screws so we could free ours up — which meant re-working
  parts we'd already built. Next time I'd plan the material budget across groups
  *before* assembly, not during.
- **Seat sizing:** confusion about how much plywood we had led us to shrink every
  component — only to find we had plenty left over. The seat still worked (and it
  was actually easier to push the driver by the shoulders anyway).

## The build

Sarah and I had fun giving the car personality — **"KACHOW"** and a lightning
bolt down the spine, painted **flames** on the axles, and **"EAT my DUST"** on
the seat-back. (I'm convinced the flames made it a little faster.)
