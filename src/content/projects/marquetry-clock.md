## Overview

A round wall clock built around a pair of **koi**. The face is stained a deep
pond blue; two calico koi — cut from **orange, cream and near-black veneer** —
chase each other nose-to-tail in a ring, and a turned wooden **lotus** sits dead
center as the hub the hands run through.

![The finished koi clock: two calico koi in orange, cream and dark-brown veneer chasing each other in a ring around a turned wooden lotus hub, set into a pond-blue stained ground.](/images/projects/marquetry-clock/clock-01.jpg)

## The koi

Each fish is **marquetry, not paint**: every colored patch — the orange
blotches, the cream body, the dark fins — is a separate piece of veneer cut to
shape and fitted edge to edge into the one beside it. The two koi mirror each
other in a yin-yang circle, tail meeting nose, so your eye keeps travelling
around the dial instead of stopping on a "top." Placing the fish this way also
leaves the numberless blue field open, which is what makes the clock read as a
pond first and a timepiece second.

## The face and the hub

The ground is stained a saturated blue to stand in for water, so the pale koi
and the warm center pop against it. At the middle, a **layered wooden lotus**
doubles as the hub cap: the clock movement passes through its heart and the slim
black hour and minute hands (plus a fine gold second hand) sweep over the fish.
Keeping the hardware minimal — no printed numerals, no bezel clutter — lets the
marquetry carry the whole face.

It was made as one of a small **koi-themed** pair, alongside a matching
laser-cut box scene on the same theme.
