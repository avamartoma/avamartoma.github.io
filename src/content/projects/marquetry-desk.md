## Overview

A **folding lap desk** I made for my brother as a graduation present. Designed in
**Fusion 360**, built in the machine shop, and surfaced in **walnut veneer over
an MDF substrate** — with the top given over to a marquetry scene of the things
he actually likes.

Reading left to right: **Hogwarts** on its cliff, the silhouette of **Florida**
(home), the **Harry Potter** monogram, **Raphael** from the Ninja Turtles,
**Lightning McQueen**, and **Optimus Prime** mid-transformation. His initials,
**JCM**, sit in the open sky in rosewood.

![The finished lap desk top: a marquetry scene in a dozen veneers — Hogwarts castle on a dark cliff, the state of Florida, the Harry Potter logo, Raphael, Lightning McQueen and Optimus Prime — with JCM inlaid in the walnut sky above.](/images/projects/marquetry-desk/desk-16.jpg)

## Why a lap desk

He wanted something he could work on in bed and then put away. So the brief was
really a mechanism problem: a rigid work surface, a **pencil groove** along the
top edge so nothing rolls off a tilted board, and **legs that fold flat** into
the underside so the whole thing stores like a tray.

The legs pivot on wooden dowels and nest between walnut rails, so folded flat the
desk is barely thicker than the top itself.

![The underside with the legs folded flat, nesting between walnut rails on dowel pivots.](/images/projects/marquetry-desk/desk-13.jpg)

![The desk standing on its unfolded legs, side on, showing how shallow it packs down.](/images/projects/marquetry-desk/desk-21.jpg)

## Building it

The substrate is **MDF**, chosen deliberately: it's dead flat and dimensionally
stable, so a large veneered field won't cup with humidity the way solid stock
can. Solid **walnut** edging wraps the panel, which is what you actually see and
touch at the edges.

![The MDF substrate on the bench with its solid walnut edging strips waiting to go on, glue bottle at the ready.](/images/projects/marquetry-desk/desk-01.jpg)

![Walnut stock on the shop bed beside my hand sketch of the folding desk.](/images/projects/marquetry-desk/desk-02.jpg)

## The marquetry

Every element is a separate piece of veneer, cut to shape and fitted edge to
edge into the field — no paint doing the work. The variety comes from the woods
themselves: pale **zebrawood and olive** for the lit castle stonework, deep
**rosewood** for the cliff and the monogram, dyed veneers for McQueen's red and
Optimus's grey and yellow.

The castle was the hard part. It's mostly straight lines and repeated
window slots at small scale, so any gap between pieces reads instantly as a
mistake — unlike the rounded characters, where the eye is more forgiving.

![Top-down detail of the marquetry: the castle's window slots in pale veneer, the Florida silhouette, and the characters fitted edge to edge.](/images/projects/marquetry-desk/desk-07.jpg)

## What I'd change

The marquetry is concentrated on the right-hand two thirds and the sky is left
mostly empty walnut. I like the negative space, but the composition leans — I'd
either push the castle further left to balance it, or commit to the emptiness and
move the monogram off centre on purpose rather than nearly-centred.
